01 / THE IDEA

One theorem, three moving parts

Bayes’ theorem connects a starting probability to evidence and a revised probability.

P(H | E)=P(E | H) × P(H)P(E)
Prior · P(H)
How plausible the hypothesis is before the new evidence.
Likelihood · P(E | H)
How expected this evidence would be if the hypothesis were true.
Evidence · P(E)
The overall chance of seeing this evidence across the possibilities considered.
Posterior · P(H | E)
The updated probability of the hypothesis after considering the evidence.
What Bayes does: updates uncertainty under stated assumptions; it does not guarantee certainty. Bayes’ theorem is one rule. Bayesian statistics is the broader approach of using probability models and updating beliefs about unknown quantities.

04 / FOLLOW ALONG

Two updates, worked through

HYPOTHETICAL TEST

A rare event and a positive result

Assume a 1% prior, 90% true-positive rate, and 5% false-positive rate. In 1,000 cases:

  • 10 have the condition; 90% of 10 = 9 true positives.
  • 990 do not; 5% of 990 = 49.5 false positives.
  • 9 ÷ (9 + 49.5) = about 15.4% probability after a positive result.

These invented numbers explain the arithmetic; they are not medical guidance or a real test’s performance.

EVERYDAY EVIDENCE

Will it rain?

Suppose the prior chance of rain is 30%. For illustration, gray clouds appear on 80% of rainy days and 25% of dry days.

  • Rain and gray clouds: 0.30 × 0.80 = 0.24.
  • Dry and gray clouds: 0.70 × 0.25 = 0.175.
  • 0.24 ÷ (0.24 + 0.175) = about 57.8% after seeing gray clouds.

The likelihoods are made-up teaching assumptions, not a local forecast.

05 / KEEP IN MIND

Common mistakes

A

Swapping the conditional

P(H | E) asks how likely the hypothesis is given evidence. P(E | H) asks how likely the evidence is if the hypothesis is true. They answer different questions and are generally not equal.

B

Forgetting the starting rate

When an event is rare, even a fairly accurate test can produce many false positives relative to true positives. The prior is part of the calculation, not an optional footnote.

C

Treating assumptions as facts

A posterior is only as useful as the model and evidence behind it. Biased samples, changing conditions, and weak measurements can make the likelihoods misleading.
